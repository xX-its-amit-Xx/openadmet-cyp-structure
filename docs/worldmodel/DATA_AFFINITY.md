# DATA_AFFINITY — measured interaction strength, every large source

Inventory for the shared-embedding biological world model. Scope: anything carrying a *measured*
number for how tightly two things bind or how fast an enzyme turns a substrate over —
Kd, Ki, IC50, EC50, AC50, kcat, Km, kcat/Km, ΔG, ΔΔG, kon/koff — plus qualitative binary binding.

Researched 2026-09-20. Counts marked **[v]** were pulled live from the source's own API/FTP on that
date. Counts marked **[p]** come from the source's publication and may lag. Counts marked **[?]**
could not be verified this pass — treat as an open item, not a fact.

## 7. Traps

**IC50 is not a constant of nature.** It depends on substrate concentration, substrate identity, enzyme concentration, pre-incubation time and readout. For CYP3A4 specifically, IC50 against midazolam, testosterone and a fluorogenic probe differ by *more than an order of magnitude on the same compound*, because 3A4 has multiple, allosterically coupled binding sites. ChEMBL and BindingDB carry all of them under `standard_type = IC50` with no way to distinguish them except by reading the assay description. **Never pool IC50 across sources. Never convert IC50 to Ki with Cheng–Prusoff unless you have [S] and Km for that exact assay** — and you almost never do.

**Unit errors are real and they are in the data you will download.** The 2025 audit of PDBbind's protein–protein subset found 3 of 262 records with mM/nM confusion — a 1000× error, sign-preserving, invisible to any outlier filter that works in log space with a wide tolerance. Ki in nM vs µM vs M is the single most common silent failure in merged affinity tables. **Assert a physical range (pKd/pKi/pIC50 ∈ [2, 13]) and inspect everything that trips it rather than dropping it.**

**Censored values ("> 10 µM") are the majority of some datasets, and imputing them creates a phantom mode.** Davis imputes ~70% of its entries at pKd = 5.0. Any model trained on it learns to predict 5.0. ChEMBL encodes censoring in `standard_relation`, which most loaders discard. A left-censored measurement is a *bound*, not a value — either model it with a censored likelihood or exclude it, but do not let `>10000 nM` become `10000 nM`.

**ChEMBL's `confidence_score` is not optional metadata.** Scores below 9 include homology-assigned targets, protein complexes, and protein families where the "target" is a guess. Boltz-2's affinity head filtered to `confidence_score = 9`, single protein, biochemical/functional assay, and went from ~3 M raw measurements to **~750 k usable pairs — a 75% loss**. Budget for that loss up front; a pipeline that keeps 3 M rows is a pipeline that did not filter.

**The same compound across splits is the default failure, and target-level leakage is worse.** A random split of ChEMBL puts analogues of the same series on both sides. But the 2026 *HonestAffinity* analysis shows something sharper: models trained with **protein-only features and no ligand at all** achieve substantial correlation with affinity labels, because each target's measurements cluster around that target's mean. Reported performance is then largely "which protein is this", not "how tightly does this ligand bind". **Split by target cluster, not by compound; and always report the protein-only baseline alongside the model.** This is the same discipline as our leave-one-TARGET-out rule in CLAUDE.md, and for the same reason.

**Activity cliffs mean RMSE is the wrong summary.** Pairs differing by one methyl and three log units are common and are exactly the pairs that matter to a chemist. A model can win on RMSE by smoothing them away. Report within-target rank correlation and cliff-pair accuracy, not just global R².

**Aggregated medians hide the thing you need to know.** Papyrus and DTC give you one number per pair. The spread that median replaced is the irreducible noise floor of the label — typically 0.5–0.8 log units for cross-lab IC50. **A model whose test RMSE is below the label noise is not better; it is leaking.** Compute the replicate spread from the raw ChEMBL rows before setting any accuracy target.

**Predicted structures labelled with real numbers are not structure-with-affinity data.** SAIR's 5.2 M structures are Boltz-1x outputs. Training a pose selector on them teaches Boltz-1x's biases — which, given FINDING 011, is the last thing this project should do. Use SAIR's *labels*; treat its *coordinates* as a prior.

**DUD-E's decoys are separable without the protein.** Chen et al. 2019: deep scorers reach near-ceiling AUC on ligand features alone. Any structure-based method validated on DUD-E has an unfalsifiable result. The same suspicion applies to every property-matched synthetic decoy set.

**Double-counting merged sources inflates every N in a grant application and every denominator in a statistic.** ChEMBL 37 now ingests 2.68 M BindingDB patent activities; BindingDB imports ChEMBL; Papyrus is built on ChEMBL 34; PLINDER annotates with both; SAIR is cofolded from both. **The union is far smaller than the sum.** Deduplicate on (InChIKey, UniProt, standard_type, value) before quoting a corpus size.

**Licence contamination propagates silently.** BioLiP2 had to delete 24,809 affinity records in January 2025 because they came from PDBbind-CN. The popular `jglaser/binding_affinity` HF set (1.9 M pairs, thousands of downloads) still merges PDBbind-CN with no licence declared. If we train on it and publish weights, we inherit that problem. **Prefer HiQBind, which was built specifically to exclude PDBbind v2020.**

**Enzyme kinetics carries its conditions in free text.** BRENDA's pH and temperature live in commentary strings. A kcat at 25 °C and a kcat at 60 °C for a thermophile are not the same quantity, and every ML kcat set except TurNuP and CatPred mixes them. For P450s add a second layer: most published CYP kinetics uses **non-physiological fluorogenic or chromogenic surrogate substrates**, so "CYP3A4 Km" often means "Km for a dye".

**LLM-extracted corpora have a new error class.** EnzyExtractDB's 218 k entries come from 137,892 PDFs and are shipped in confidence tiers precisely because the extractor misreads units and mis-attributes substrates across multi-panel tables. Use the high-confidence tier only, and spot-check.

**PubChem "inactive" is not "non-binder".** It means inactive in one assay at one concentration. Training a binary binder classifier on PubChem inactives teaches assay-specific interference, not non-binding.

## 8. Acquisition order

Given the above, the order that maximises usable signal per GB and per legal risk:

1. **ChEMBL 37** SQLite dump — 24.5 M activities, ~6 GB gz, CC BY-SA 3.0. The backbone. Filter to `confidence_score = 9` + single protein immediately.
2. **BindingDB TSV** — 3.2 M measurements, ~1.5 GB, CC BY 4.0. Deduplicate against ChEMBL on InChIKey × UniProt × type.
3. **HiQBind** — >30,000 clean pose+affinity complexes, ~10 GB, MIT/figshare. The licence-clean PDBbind replacement; this is the structure-with-affinity source we should actually build on.
4. **BioLiP2** — 989,724 entries, 45,432 with affinity, free, BSD. Binding-residue annotations are the part PDBbind never had.
5. **SAIR parquet only** (`sair.parquet`, not the 812 GB of structures) — ~1.05 M labelled pairs, CC BY 4.0.

Then, per modality: **BRENDA textfile** (71.6 MB, CC BY 4.0) and **CatPred-DB** (96 MB, CC BY 4.0) for kinetics; **SKEMPI 2.0** (1.6 MB, CC BY 4.0) for PPI; **ProNAB** for protein–NA; **OpenADMET CYP challenge + Octant** for the CYP slice.

Storage note per CLAUDE.md: everything above except the ChEMBL dump and HiQBind is small enough to keep local. The ChEMBL dump, HiQBind and anything from SAIR must go to `onedrive:` via `cypstruct.storage.push()` — **not** through the `O:\` VFS mount, which caches to the near-full C: drive.
